The ferry pulls away from Pier 33.

The city shrinks behind you. Fifteen minutes later you step onto Alcatraz.

You walk the cellhouse with headphones on while former guards and inmates describe life on the island. You stand in a cell. You look out through the bars at San Francisco, a little over a mile away. On quiet nights the inmates could hear the city from here.

Why pick one boat when you can take two?

Back on the mainland, you grab lunch near the wharf and board again, this time for a cruise straight under the Golden Gate. By mid-afternoon you've seen Alcatraz from the inside and the bridge from below. You're windswept and very pleased with yourself.

In the Mission, the air smells like roasting cacao.

Dandelion Chocolate makes its bars from two ingredients: cacao beans and cane sugar. Their factory is a few blocks away, and you're invited inside.

Tim's Big BirthdayChapter two

Afternoon · Bean to bar

A guide hands you a cacao bean.

It tastes nothing like chocolate yet. By the end of the hour, it will.

You follow the beans across the factory floor: sacks from single farms, the roaster, the machine that cracks them and blows the shells away, the stone grinders that slowly turn nibs into chocolate. You taste it at every stage, and you leave with a bag of bars that may or may not survive the walk to dinner.

After the tour, someone hands you an apron.
